Selected Publications

GRAPE: Graph-Augmented Prototype Explanations for Interactive Medical Image Diagnosis

Rasul Khanbayov, Erchin Serpedin, Hasan Kurban
Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026
GRAPE is an interactive prototype-based classifier for medical images that models anatomical concept co-occurrence with a graph attention head, warns when physician feedback conflicts with the model's evidence, and adds new findings from a single labeled image.

IRIS: A Real-World Benchmark for Inverse Recovery and Identification of Physical Dynamic Systems from Monocular Video

Rasul Khanbayov*, Mohamed Rayan Barhdadi*, Erchin Serpedin, Hasan Kurban (* equal contribution)
European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026
IRIS is a benchmark of 240 real-world 4K, 60 fps videos of single- and multi-body dynamical systems with measured ground-truth parameters, plus a standardized protocol for recovering physical parameters and governing equations from monocular video.

Consistency Has a Computable Blind Spot: A Commutation Theory of Label-Free Reliability for Vision–Language Figure Reading

Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban
Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL, 2027
Shows that the blind spot of invariance-based reliability checks for figure-reading vision-language models is computable, and introduces the Equivariance-Consistency Score, a label-free detector built on edits that should change the answer by a known amount.

When Does Consensus Mean Correctness? Measuring the Agreement–Accuracy Coupling with Semantics-Preserving Re-Rendering

Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban
Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL, 2027
Uses programmatically re-rendered scientific figures to measure when a vision-language model's agreement across equivalent inputs actually tracks correctness, and shows that fine-tuning on self-consensus lowers accuracy.

Truncation Samplers Are Nested: Comparing Decoding Rules Is a Measurement Problem

Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban
Submitted to ICLR 2027 · arXiv:2610.15570
Shows that almost all deployed truncation samplers keep a prefix of the probability-sorted vocabulary and are therefore nested, which yields a closed-form separation certificate and a coupling that gives an unbiased, lower-variance paired estimator for comparing decoding rules.

The Gradient That Top-k Distillation Never Computes

Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban
Submitted to AISTATS 2027 · arXiv:2610.15589
Proves that renormalized top-k distillation has exactly zero gradient along the directions that control the teacher's tail mass, so a student's tail mass can drift away from the teacher's, and measures the effect on seven open-weight teachers.

A Low Grounding Score Is Not an Ungrounded Judge: Identifying the Perceptibility Confound in Multimodal Oversight

Rasul Khanbayov, Hasan Kurban
Submitted to AAAI 2027 (AI Alignment Track) · arXiv:2610.00111
Shows that counterfactual grounding probes for multimodal model judges are capped by how perceptible the image edit is, so a low score can be a false alarm, and identifies the false-alarm rate from quantities the audit already collects.

One Perturbation Is Not Enough: Identifiability and Blind Baselines for Behavioral AI Evaluation

Rasul Khanbayov, Mariam Sohail, Ahmed Abdala, Hasan Kurban

Proves how many independent perturbations a behavioral evaluation needs to certify that a model uses an input, derives the score a blind policy would receive, and finds that three vision-language models reading physical quantities from video score below their own blind bound.
